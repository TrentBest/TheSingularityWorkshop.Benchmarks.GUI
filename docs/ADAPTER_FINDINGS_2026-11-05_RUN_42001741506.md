## 1. Executive summary

1. **Blazor render-tree construction is workload-dependent.** GUI.Blazor was about 8.2% slower at 2 sections, about 1.6% faster at 6 sections, and about 12.1% faster at 18 sections in this run.
2. **GUI.Blazor allocated more managed memory at every size.** At 18 sections, 18.44 KB versus 14.56 KB native (about 26.6% more).
3. **WPF lifecycle timings were generally close to native.** Construction was about 5.3% slower at 6 sections and 3.1% slower at 18 sections; the small workload happened to favor GUI.WPF. Other phase differences were mixed and mostly modest.
4. **WPF construction allocations were consistently higher:** about 15–20% across the three workloads.
5. **The browser-hosted static comparison completed and its CSV passed structural validation:** exactly 60 rows, 30 native and 30 GUI. Browser measurements are retained in the raw artifact. This report does not repeat browser timing medians because the workflow console validates the CSV but does not emit its measurement values; extract and analyze the artifact before making a new browser-performance claim.

The conclusion is not “GUI is faster” or “GUI is slower.” The adapter overhead depends on workload size and lifecycle phase. The large Blazor render-tree case is encouraging, but the allocation trade-off remains real.

## 2. Blazor render-tree construction

These are BenchmarkDotNet means for .NET-side render-tree construction. They do **not** measure browser parsing, layout, paint, interactive startup, or event handling.

| Sections | Native mean | GUI mean | GUI/native | Native allocated | GUI allocated | Allocation ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 1.141 μs | 1.235 μs | 1.08x | 1.99 KB | 3.08 KB | 1.55x |
| 6 | 3.362 μs | 3.308 μs | 0.98x | 6.68 KB | 8.69 KB | 1.30x |
| 18 | 9.344 μs | 8.213 μs | 0.88x | 14.56 KB | 18.44 KB | 1.27x |

At 18 sections, GUI.Blazor took 1.131 μs less per measured operation (about 12.1% less time) while allocating 3.88 KB more (about 26.6%). BenchmarkDotNet recorded 15 native observations and 14 GUI observations for this case; one GUI outlier was removed by BenchmarkDotNet's outlier handling. The 99.9% confidence intervals for the means were 9.247–9.441 μs native and 8.094–8.331 μs GUI in this process. This is a tight within-run estimate, but independent hosted runs are still required to establish repeatability.

At 2 sections, GUI was slower (1.235 vs 1.141 μs). At 6 sections, it was marginally faster (3.308 vs 3.362 μs). This changing relationship is why the result must remain size-specific.

## 6. What this run establishes—and what it does not

### Supported by this artifact

- Blazor render-tree timing varies by workload size; the largest tested GUI case was faster in this run.
- GUI.Blazor allocated more managed memory at every tested size.
- WPF lifecycle phase timings were generally near native, with mixed wins/losses.
- WPF construction allocated more managed memory in the GUI case at every size.
- Browser CSV completeness was validated at 30 samples per implementation.

### Not established

- That GUI.Blazor is generally faster, or that the large-workload result will repeat on another run or machine.
- Statistical significance across independent hosted runs.
- Interactive Blazor event latency, full application startup, or user-perceived responsiveness.
- Visible WPF paint or physical-input latency.
- Retained heap size, sustained GC impact, or total process working set.
- Performance across other hardware, runtimes, browsers, or workload compositions.

## 8. Engineering handoff

**Current interpretation:** GUI adapters are competitive with native implementations in these measured scenarios, but the cost profile is workload- and phase-dependent. The largest Blazor render-tree case is encouraging (8.213 μs GUI vs 9.344 μs native), paired with a higher allocation cost (18.44 KB vs 14.56 KB). WPF lifecycle results are mostly near parity; construction allocation remains a clear target.

**Recommended next question:** can the adapter's validation and rendering pipeline reduce allocations while preserving correctness and the favorable large-workload Blazor timing?
